# The Blunt Test

**Live:** https://vishnuvsdevtech.github.io/blunt-test/

48 questions for AI engineering, scored by **evidence rather than confidence**. The
only answer that counts as a full point is one you could show somebody: a metric, an
eval file, a trace, or a run that failed and stopped.

## Why

Ask a hundred developers using AI to improve an output and most will change the prompt
and eyeball the result. A small number will write thirty test cases with expected
answers, count passes, and only then change anything. That habit — a tiny eval set — is
the largest single divider between people who use AI and people who can be trusted with
it. This page is that difference, turned into questions you can answer.

## Three states per question

| Mark | Meaning | Points |
|---|---|---|
| no | you cannot answer it | 0 |
| yes, unprovable | you could talk about it but not show it | 1 |
| yes + artefact | you could open the thing in an interview | 2 |

"I could knock one out" is amber, not green. That distinction is the whole instrument.

## What it produces

- A score out of 96, and a blunt band from *competing on vocabulary* to *could lead this*
- **A build plan**: the five weakest questions, each with the smallest artefact that
  would turn it green — most are an afternoon
- **An export**: Markdown checklist of all 48 with your marks and evidence notes

Progress saves in the browser only. Nothing leaves the device.

## The eight sections

Measurement · Retrieval & data · Model behaviour · Agents & tooling · Cost & latency ·
Production & safety · Domain & product · Evidence & craft

## Honest limits

It is a self-assessment, not a certificate. The score is only as honest as the person
marking it, which is why every green requires a note saying what you would show. The
value is the list and the plan, not the number.

## Using it in a job search

Work it top to bottom and build the five artefacts. Then the interview answer to
*"what have you shipped with AI?"* stops being a description and becomes a demo, and
*"what happens when it fails?"* stops being a hypothetical.
